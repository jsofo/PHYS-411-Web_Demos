Airy Functions

Airy Functions as Solutions of the Schrödinger Equation

Exact solutions vs. asymptotic (WKB) approximations · interactive comparison

Physical context

Consider a quantum particle in a linear potential $V(x) = Fx$ (constant force, e.g. a uniform electric field). The time-independent Schrödinger equation reads

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + Fx\,\psi = E\,\psi$$

Defining the dimensionless variable $z = \bigl(\frac{2mF}{\hbar^2}\bigr)^{1/3}\!\bigl(x - \tfrac{E}{F}\bigr)$, the equation reduces exactly to the Airy equation:

$$\frac{d^2 y}{dz^2} - z\,y = 0$$

The two linearly independent solutions are $\operatorname{Ai}(z)$ and $\operatorname{Bi}(z)$. The physically relevant solution for a bound state (e.g. a particle confined by a turning point) is $\operatorname{Ai}(z)$, which decays for $z\to+\infty$ and oscillates for $z\to-\infty$. The function $\operatorname{Bi}(z)$ grows exponentially for $z\to+\infty$ and is excluded by normalisability.

Asymptotic expressions

$z \to +\infty$  (classically forbidden)

Ai(z)
$$\operatorname{Ai}(z)\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\,z^{-1/4}\,e^{-\zeta}, \quad \zeta=\tfrac{2}{3}z^{3/2}$$
Bi(z)
$$\operatorname{Bi}(z)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,z^{-1/4}\,e^{+\zeta}$$

$z \to -\infty$  (classically allowed)

Ai($-t$),  $t>0$
$$\operatorname{Ai}(-t)\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,t^{-1/4}\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}t^{3/2}-\tfrac{\pi}{4}\Bigr)$$
Bi($-t$),  $t>0$
$$\operatorname{Bi}(-t)\sim-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,t^{-1/4}\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}t^{3/2}-\tfrac{\pi}{4}\Bigr)$$
WKB connection. For $z\ll -1$ the asymptotic forms are exactly the WKB wavefunctions $\psi\propto |p(x)|^{-1/2}\exp\!\bigl(\pm\tfrac{i}{\hbar}\int|p|\,dx\bigr)$ evaluated near the turning point. The phase $\tfrac{2}{3}t^{3/2}-\tfrac{\pi}{4}$ encodes the Maslov index $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$ per turning point. The Airy function thus provides the uniform approximation that patches the WKB solutions across the classically forbidden region.
