The orbital and the contour

ψ2pₓ(x,y,z) = [1 / (4√(2π) a₀3/2)] (x/a₀) e−r/(2a₀),   r = √(x² + y² + z²).

This is the normalized, real hydrogen 2pₓ wavefunction. Its two signs are shown in different colors; these are wavefunction phases, not positive and negative charge. The probability density |ψ|² is nonnegative in both lobes.

A contour is a surface on which the selected quantity is constant. The percentage specifies a fraction of its peak value, not the probability enclosed by the surface. A nonzero contour has two separate closed lobes. The orbital extends beyond them and vanishes throughout the yz plane (x = 0).

The surfaces are calculated from the wavefunction, using its rotational symmetry about x. At a relative amplitude c, the positive lobe satisfies r/a₀ = 2 ln[e(x/a₀)/(2c)]. Density contours use c = √(relative density). The viewer needs no external libraries, assets, or network connection.
